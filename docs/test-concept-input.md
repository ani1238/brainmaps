NCERT curriculum points:
- The 7 characteristics of living things: nutrition, respiration, growth, reproduction, movement, response to stimuli, excretion
- Distinction between living and non-living things
- Plants are living things even though they don't walk
- Seeds appear non-living but are alive (dormant)
- Some non-living things mimic one or two characteristics (e.g. fire grows, cars move)

Key concepts for FEYNMAN keyConcepts + BLURT keyPoints:
- nutrition (all living things need food/energy)
- respiration (release of energy from food — not just breathing)
- growth (increase in size and complexity over time)
- reproduction (producing offspring — essential for continuation of species)
- movement (includes non-locomotory movement like plant bending toward light)
- response to stimuli (reacting to changes in environment)
- excretion (removal of waste products)
- dormancy (alive but metabolically inactive — seeds, hibernating animals)
- all 7 characteristics together define life — no single one is sufficient

Common misconceptions to probe:
- Movement = locomotion: students think plants aren't alive because they don't walk
- Fire is alive because it grows and needs oxygen
- Seeds are non-living because they look like stones and don't move
- Cars/robots are alive because they move and need fuel
- Breathing = respiration (respiration is a cellular process; breathing is just one mechanism)
- One characteristic is enough to call something living (e.g. "fire eats so it's alive")
